# estimate: what a project costs, line by line

Use for "how much does X cost" topics where the reader's own size, scope or quality changes the answer (renovation, wedding, event, moving house, starting a business). Worked example: agent/examples/estimate.json. The schema is `EstimateSpecSchema` in lib/spec.ts.

Top level: `"kind": "estimate"`, `id`, `title`, `subject`, `currency`, `priceYear`, `scope`, `limits`, `inputs`, `tiers`, `contingency`, `groups`, `lines`. Optional: `presets`, `providers`.

- `inputs`: 0 to 4 numbers the viewer sets, e.g. floor area or guests `{id, label, unit, default, min, max, step, rationale, choices}`. Defaults must be typical; min and max sensible limits. `choices` are quick picks `[{label, value}]`, e.g. flat types that set the floor area.
- `tiers`: quality levels `{label, default, options: [{id, label, note, rationale}]}`, 2 to 3 of them (e.g. basic, standard, premium). Omit for a single level; then each line's `prices` has one key, `"default"`.
- `contingency`: `{default, max}` as fractions, e.g. 0.1 and 0.3.
- `groups`: 3 to 12 `{id, label}` that split the total.
- `lines`: 8 to 16 items, or up to 60 when the request asks for every category `{id, group, label, description, per, unit, qty, qtyMax, optional, prices, source, notes, rationale}`. `per` is an input id (price is per unit of that input) or "fixed" (price per unit and the viewer sets `qty`, e.g. 2 bathrooms). `unit` says what one unit is ("per sqm", "per room"). `prices` has one `{low, high}` range per tier id, for ONE unit; low is a cheap but realistic quote, high a dear one. Mark `optional: true` for things many people skip; they start switched off.
- `presets` (when the job comes in kinds, e.g. full BTO renovation, resale renovation, kitchen only): `{label, default, options: [{id, label, note, rationale, lines: [line ids switched on]}]}`. The chosen preset decides which lines start on; the viewer can still tick any line.
- `providers` (when the reader picks who does the work): `{label, default, maxYear, options: [{id, name, type, url, factor, factorBasis, history, rationale, source, notes}]}`. `factor` is the provider's price level against the line ranges (1 = market, 1.15 = 15% dearer); `factorBasis` says which of its prices were compared with which market figure. `history` is at least three years of the provider's OWN prices for the same scope, oldest first `[{year, value, label, url}]` (e.g. its 4-room package price each year, from its site, an archived copy of its site at web.archive.org, or dated reviews and quotes); the page grows its prices at the compound yearly rate across that history to price a job in a later year. Include a "Market average" option (factor 1) whose history is a published market-wide figure. Where a provider's own figure for a year can't be found, say so in `rationale`, use the nearest dated figure, and mark the source type "estimate".
- `rationale` (lines, inputs, tiers, presets, providers): one or two sentences on why the figure is what it is and anything assumed. It is shown as hover text, so every assumption must have one.
- Every price is a range from real sources. Where a source gives only a whole-project range, split it across lines yourself, say so in `limits` and the line's `rationale`, and give the line `"type": "estimate"` (shown as "AI estimate").
